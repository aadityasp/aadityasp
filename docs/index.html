Questions people ask before they start

Do I qualify?

Eligibility depends on your state, age, BMI, and health history; a licensed provider makes the final call after intake.

What is the $49?

Your first month on the monthly plan, which then renews at $135. The 3- and 6-month plans are billed once per term.

Can I cancel?

Yes, anytime before your next renewal date from account settings.

What if treatment is not right for me?

Provider review happens before treatment starts; if care is not appropriate, you are refunded in full.

Is medication included in the price?

Membership covers clinical care, provider review, and care team support. Any medication cost is shown before you pay.

Ready when you are.

Prescription products require an online consultation with a licensed healthcare provider, who determines whether a prescription is appropriate. Individual results vary. Availability varies by state.

Why the flow is structured this way

Trust and comprehension are treated as conversion inputs: each page has one job, and price language stays consistent from ad to checkout.

Land

Connect the ad promise to clinical support, process, and a visible plan range.

Measure: CTA engagement

Understand

Clarify what is included and how licensed provider review works.

Measure: qualified starts

Compare

Show all terms with the same price anatomy before asking for an account.

Measure: plan comprehension

Commit

Carry the choice into checkout and offer financing without unverified promises.

Measure: checkout completion
Move pricing before account creation

Reason: people evaluate the offer before giving personal information.

Risk to test: fewer early account captures for retargeting.

Confirm eligibility before showing plans

Reason: licensing and BMI decide whether care exists at all, so ask first.

Risk to test: early personal questions may lower continuation.

Say “Recommended for you” and show why

Reason: it follows the priority the person picked, not a “best value” claim.

Risk to test: any label may anchor; compare against none.

Quote savings against the $135 rate

Reason: the $49 offer makes monthly cheaper for a first term ($319 vs $349) and narrows the 6-month saving to $125.

Risk to test: price-aware users will do the math; consider a first-term credit on prepaid plans.

Keep total and monthly equivalent together

Reason: supports comparison while preserving the amount due.

Risk to test: the equivalent may read as an installment.

Add Affirm as an optional path

Reason: softens the heavier upfront cost of longer plans.

Risk to test: eligibility and APR language must come from Affirm.

Capture auto-renewal consent at checkout

Reason: renewal price and cadence sit beside the total with an explicit checkbox, as negative-option rules expect.

Risk to test: extra friction; compare against disclosure only.

State medication-price uncertainty

Reason: the brief prices membership, not medication.

Production need: confirm inclusions, renewals, refunds, and financing terms.

06

Measure and roll out

Three plan lengths change cash flow, refunds, and retention, not only conversion. The rollout has to prove pricing-first earns more paying members without a refund problem later.

Primary metric

Net revenue per landing visitor

Measured at 90 days, after refunds. Secondary: paid conversion, plan mix with a target of 35% or more on 3- or 6-month terms, first-payment value, and Affirm take rate.

Guardrails

Any breach pauses rollout

Rollout pauses if paid conversion drops more than 10% relative, 14-day refunds rise more than 2 points, chargebacks pass 0.5%, or billing and cancellation contacts per 100 orders rise.

Experiments

Three tests, in order

1. Pricing-first against the current account-first flow, split by traffic source, two weeks or 1,000 first payments per arm. 2. “Recommended for you” label against none. 3. Affirm hints on prepaid plans only against all plans.

Open questions

Decisions needed before launch

Medication cost inside membership or not, the refund rule when a provider declines treatment, whether $49 applies only to monthly, today’s median monthly tenure, Affirm terms, and day-one state coverage.

What the three terms do for the business

1Cash up front

A 6-month prepay collects $599 on day one against $49 on monthly, so acquisition cost is recovered at checkout.

2Fewer churn moments

Monthly carries five renewal decisions in six months; a 6-month plan carries none. A monthly member who leaves after four payments is worth $454, a 6-month member $599.

3Refund exposure

Prepay creates a refund liability and deferred revenue: $599 times the ineligible-after-payment rate times the prepaid share.

4The intro offer sets the mix

The $49 first month makes monthly the cheapest first term, so the mix skews monthly unless prepaid plans earn it.
